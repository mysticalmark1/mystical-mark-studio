## Instructions to the assistant

Research the news thoroughly and assess my forecasts against the attached advance register. Use the supplied creator profile and transcript for context; do not invent identity, qualifications, website or channel details. Write the public article in my warm, reflective first-person voice while keeping reporting, intuitive interpretation and research judgments distinct.

The goal is an honest, readable examination of what happened—including contrary evidence, absent corroboration and continuing outcomes. Do not seek to prove me right or disprove me by default. Judge only what the register actually says.

### 1. Establish the assessment baseline before searching

Choose weekly or monthly mode from my request; default to standalone weekly mode for one weekly register unless I specify otherwise. Read every in-scope supplied register and record in full. In weekly mode, assess the current register only; do not require or fetch earlier weekly records. In monthly mode, reconcile this month’s registers/results and the incoming monthly tracker. Only retrieve an older original record if a particular missing or conflicting criterion prevents fair assessment; request that specific record if unavailable, not the whole archive. Use the original transcript to resolve wording when available. If it is unavailable, work from the register's preserved excerpts and clearly state that limit. Never imply you checked a full video if you only read its transcript.

Identify the period, research cutoff, register version, recording/publication evidence, extraction timing, known outcome exposure and whether the creator’s review was recorded. Preserve all original claim IDs and criteria. An extracted baseline is not automatically an approved or independently certified preregistration.

If the window is still open, produce an interim review and keep open claims Pending. Use the timezone in the creator profile (UTC if unspecified) for review dates; retain local event times/timezones where relevant. Distinguish publication dates from event dates and identify later reporting about in-window events.

Do not silently repair a post-outcome register into a prospective one. If there is no original register, do useful retrospective analysis, but label it retrospectively extracted rather than advance-registered. If a rule is materially ambiguous, explain how that limits resolution; do not choose whichever interpretation creates a match.

Create the full in-scope claim index before news searching. In weekly mode, cross-week deduplication is explicitly deferred to month-end; do not invent links to unavailable records. In monthly mode, account for all current-month claims and all incoming tracker entries, showing carried claims separately from new-month claims. Assess all registered externally assessable claims, including awkward or weak ones. Preserve advice/non-assessable categories separately. Do not choose a denominator after finding the best stories.

### 2. Follow the register's evidence plan

Conduct broad discovery appropriate to the geographic and subject scope, including relevant local reporting. Prioritise official records and original, reliable reporting. News research must include support, contradiction and prior-background checks for every assessable claim. Search more deeply when sources conflict or a claim is serious; do not substitute arbitrary source counts for quality.

Read the actual source wherever possible. Do not treat a headline, search summary or AI-generated summary as enough for a complicated allegation. If access is limited, find another reliable account or disclose the limitation and lower confidence. Never fabricate a source, title, date, URL or outcome. Reports syndicated across sites are not independent confirmations.

Keep separate:

- Proposal, announcement, implementation and demonstrated effect.
- Allegation, investigation, finding and conviction.
- Disclosure and actual accountability or remedy.
- Calls for resignation and completed removal.
- Old underlying conduct and a new public report about it.
- Institutional action and claims about private intention.
- An already announced or scheduled development and a novel occurrence.

Check what was publicly known before the recording. Relevant existing trends, scheduled meetings and earlier policy announcements reduce novelty even if the later event fits. Label background checks performed now as retrospective checks.

Record a concise research log with search date, claim ID, representative support/contrary/background queries, sources consulted and access limitations. A global search is not exhaustive; do not claim exhaustive coverage or prove worldwide absence from a failure to find a story.

### 3. Resolve claims using the same categories every time

Preserve the register's windows and criteria. Give each claim one current overall status and separately describe its components. For a linked sequence, the entire sequence is needed for full support. A new endpoint, expanded geography, extra tolerance or later outcome cannot silently rescue an expired prediction.

| Status | Apply when |
|---|---|
| **Supported under registered criteria** | All essential observable criteria and recorded timing are met. This does not establish above-chance skill or causation. |
| **Partially supported** | Meaningful components fit but important components are missing or materially different. |
| **Contradicted** | Reliable evidence conflicts with a definite stated expectation. |
| **Window expired without corroboration** | The window closed and a documented, adequate search did not establish qualifying evidence. This is not proof of worldwide non-occurrence. |
| **Pending** | The original horizon remains open. Log observations without presenting a final resolution. |
| **Unresolved—insufficient evidence** | Source gaps, ambiguity, an untriggered condition or other limitations prevent a fair resolution. State the reason. |
| **Not externally assessable** | The statement lacks a workable observable proposition or concerns inaccessible private/spiritual states. |

Do not use lack of research as an expired-without-corroboration finding. Do not turn “may happen” into “will happen.” An untriggered if–then claim is untested, not successful. A status change must have a reason and date.

For each assessed claim, separately report:

- **Subject fit:** Direct, partial, absent or unclear; explain the concrete overlap.
- **Timing fit:** Core window, explicitly registered extended window, outside window or uncertain.
- **Original specificity:** What was actually named versus left unspecified. Do not score the specificity of the later news story as if it came from the forecast.
- **Novelty/background:** Previously public, scheduled, genuinely new development or unknown. Do not infer what the creator privately knew.
- **Evidence confidence:** High, moderate or low, referring to the reliability/completeness of event evidence—not probability that astrology works.
- **Contrary evidence and missing components:** Include them even when some elements fit.

Avoid a single “close match” label that hides these distinctions. When the register contains broad themes, distinguish an illustrative resemblance from evidence of increased frequency or intensity. If no appropriate baseline was defined and measured, say so. Do not invent an accuracy percentage, statistical significance or a ranking against other astrologers.

### 4. Track longer transits and repeated stories fairly

In weekly mode, give cases register-prefixed IDs to avoid collisions. Mark cross-week identity reconciliation as deferred rather than claiming a story is globally new. In monthly mode, assign or reuse canonical event/case IDs and preserve an alias map for weekly IDs so none are silently lost. Assess developments involving older carried claims here, not as a routine weekly obligation.

Assign stable event/case IDs in the assessment record. Link multiple claims to one event without presenting it as several independent successful events. Repeated stories are updates, not fresh confirmations. Preserve distinct claim IDs when their promises differ; distinguish number of claims from number of unique events.

For a monthly review, retain the original weekly windows. Synthesize overlap and progression without replacing them with a month-long window. A later event may bear on a separately registered longer claim while the short claim remains uncorroborated. Identify opening spillover from the prior month and follow-through into the next month separately from the main month’s results.

For open long-term claims, state what has happened so far, what remains necessary and the existing deadline. If the original claim had no finite deadline, keep it as open-ended thematic tracking; do not manufacture a final success. Preserve earlier status history and explain changes with new evidence.

Put unregistered connections in a clearly separate **Exploratory observations** section. They may be interesting and useful, but they do not enter the registered results. Subsequent methodology improvements apply prospectively, not retroactively.

### 5. Create a website-ready Markdown article

Use the following stable structure. Keep the prose human and engaging; explain technical terms briefly. Avoid competitive “wins” when discussing suffering. Respect my spiritual framing without asserting that news establishes a higher-self source, planetary causation or scientific validity.

1. **SEO/editorial block:** Suggested page title, meta description, slug and short excerpt, clearly separated from article copy.
2. **Title and substantial opening:** The period’s actual findings and mixed outcomes; no automatic success narrative. Include review date, coverage period and cutoff.
3. **Concise disclosure and method:** AI-assisted research/drafting; actual register timing/review status; assessable claims distinguished from spiritual guidance. Do not claim I personally checked every source or approved a draft unless I have. Explain that support under criteria is not proof of predictive ability.
4. **Original forecasts:** Forecast dates, supplied/verified recording and publication details, clickable video links and an optional embed placeholder. Mark metadata limitations plainly.
5. **Assessment overview:** A readable table of every assessed claim ID, short expectation, original window, status and brief reason. Group for readability without dropping claims. List non-assessable items separately. Any counts must reconcile, and must not be marketed as an accuracy rate.
6. **Detailed comparisons:** Organise by meaningful story or theme. Show original quotation and timestamp; registered expectation/window; what happened with event dates and nearby source links; subject/timing fit; original specificity; prior background; contrary evidence; final status and rationale. Include expired or contradicted items even when no dramatic story exists. Cross-reference all IDs if a section covers several claims.
7. **Chronology:** For weekly reviews, a day-by-day view; for monthly reviews, a compact month chronology. Do not fill quiet dates with weak matches. Say “no assessed development identified” rather than asserting nothing happened.
8. **Longer-transit follow-up:** In weekly articles, list only current-register claims that remain open and their original windows, noting that cross-week follow-through is reviewed monthly. In monthly articles, reconcile current and carried claims, new developments, unchanged deadlines and still-open criteria. Explain recurring themes without double-counting events. A monthly reporting cadence does not extend any original event window; later research may establish an in-window event, with both event and reporting dates shown.
9. **Exploratory observations:** Only if useful; visibly outside registered results.
10. **Limits, corrections and reflection:** What fit, what did not, what is unresolved and what could be improved in future practice. Preserve inconvenient findings. End with an optional gentle invitation to inspect the original videos and share perspectives, using my usual purple-heart tone without turning anecdotes into evidence.
11. **Linked sources:** Titles, publishers, dates and working direct URLs; match each substantive factual claim to its supporting source. Include the register reference or supplied public link if one exists. Do not invent a public URL for a private file.

Use ordinary Markdown links that survive copying into my website editor. Use timestamp links based on the supplied SRT and verified/supplied video URL. Do not leave platform-only citation tokens as the article’s sole references. Keep internal queries and unresolved production questions out of the public article; disclose material evidence limits in readable prose.

### 6. Create a separate assessment record

Create `Mystical_Mark_Assessment_Record_YYYY-MM-DD_to_YYYY-MM-DD.md` alongside `Mystical_Mark_Forecast_vs_News_YYYY-MM-DD_to_YYYY-MM-DD.md`.

The assessment record must be self-contained. Weekly records are saved for that month’s synthesis, not carried into each following week. The full monthly record is archived; only its compact tracker is a routine input for the next month. Include:

- Workflow version, reviewed register filenames/versions/IDs, assessment date and cutoff.
- Each original claim ID, exact passage reference, immutable original criteria/window, current status, component findings, fit dimensions, evidence URLs, event IDs and reasoning.
- A status history with dates and reasons, retaining earlier findings when supplied.
- A unique-event index linking claims and distinguishing new cases from updates.
- A register of Pending, Unresolved and open-ended claims with review dates where defined.
- Separate exploratory matches, factual-background corrections and methodology amendments.
- The concise research log and access gaps.
- Reconciled coverage totals: assessed claims, pending/unresolved/non-assessable items and unique cases. Explain duplicates/related claims; no improvised accuracy rate.

Preserve the original advance register. Put results and later explanations in this record, not into a silently rewritten baseline. Keep any new operational decisions dated and disclose their post-outcome status.

### 7. Monthly mode only: produce the compact carry-forward tracker

Create `Mystical_Mark_Carry_Forward_YYYY-MM.md` for the month just reviewed. This is a self-contained handoff, not a fourth public article or a cumulative archive. Do not create or update it in weekly mode unless I explicitly request an exception.

Include only claims still needing future review: genuinely open longer-horizon claims; bounded unresolved cases with a concrete evidence gap or next check; and a short, separate list of open-ended themes. Do not carry resolved successes, contradictions or expired uncorroborated short-window claims as active predictions. A concluded case may appear only if a specific outstanding correction/evidence issue requires follow-up, clearly marked as such, without reopening its original window.

Use a small index followed by compact entries. Preserve necessary evidence even if that makes the tracker longer than one page; brevity must not change the criteria. For each active entry include:

- Original claim ID(s), origin register filename/version and source video/timestamp.
- Exact relevant forecast wording, retaining qualifications; all operative support, partial, contradiction and insufficient-evidence rules copied unchanged from the baseline, including whether any were assistant-proposed or remained unapproved.
- Scope, core and extended windows, original deadline or explicitly undefined endpoint; original extraction/prospective status and any material dated amendments.
- Latest status, as-of date, brief evidence summary, key direct source links and canonical event IDs/weekly aliases where needed.
- What remains unresolved, the next concrete evidence check, and the next monthly review checkpoint. Keep the administrative review date separate from the original event deadline.
- A reference to the full originating register and latest assessment record for optional deeper inspection. Do not fabricate public links; exact filenames/IDs are sufficient.

Retain original distinct claims where criteria differ; related claims can share a case summary. No arbitrary cap may hide unresolved claims. Put open-ended themes in their own non-scored watchlist. Review their continuing usefulness monthly; archive inactive or non-assessable entries with a reason, without calling them successful or imposing a retrospective prediction deadline. Similarly, do not keep evidence-gap cases active forever without a concrete reason: when further research is not reasonably available, archive as Unresolved with the limitation preserved.

Reconcile every incoming tracker ID as retained, resolved, archived unresolved, or linked as a duplicate with original IDs retained. Put the full transition log in the monthly assessment record. In the outgoing tracker, include only a short **Closed or archived this month** list of IDs, final statuses and reasons from this review cycle; it is not active input next month and must not accumulate indefinitely.

Record tracker month/version, creation date, covered-through cutoff and source records. If nothing remains active, still provide a short tracker stating that fact. If an incoming tracker is missing, create one from available current-month material and disclose that older continuity was not checked; do not invent an empty historical backlog. A complete incoming tracker should be sufficient for routine carried-claim review without attaching all older files. Flag only specific missing baseline details that prevent resolution.

### 8. Final checks and delivery

Before delivery, check that every registered ID is accounted for, quotations/timestamps match the source, no timing rule moved silently, sources support the actual wording, and the article and assessment record agree. Confirm that allegations, conditional claims and uncertain findings are not overstated. Verify that your conclusions reflect contrary evidence rather than simply adding a disclaimer beneath a success narrative.

Deliver two files in weekly mode and three in monthly mode, with a short summary of the findings. Weekly handoff: save this register and assessment record for month-end; next week needs only its own register. Monthly handoff: retain the full monthly record in the archive and attach only the new carry-forward tracker alongside next month’s weekly registers/results. Check that every incoming tracker ID is reconciled and that outgoing entries preserve original criteria and deadlines. Do not require a growing stack of prior monthly or weekly records. If an attachment is missing, complete all well-supported work possible and identify the exact limitation. Do not invent a complete audit or promise automatic future follow-up.
